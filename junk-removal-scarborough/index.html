Scarborough Disposal & Pricing Questions

Does the City pick up large furniture and appliances?

Yes. The City of Toronto collects oversized items such as appliances, furniture and carpets on your regular garbage collection day, set out 0.5 metres (2 feet) from your bins and at the curb by 7:00 a.m. Bulky furniture like couches and mattresses does not need to be dismantled, but you must remove the doors from fridges, ovens and similar appliances first. Verified against the City of Toronto Oversized & Metal Items page, July 2026.

What won't the City take from the curb?

Construction and renovation waste — drywall, wood, and similar debris — is not collected at the curb, and neither is household hazardous waste such as paint tins and fluorescent tubes. Those must go to a City Drop-Off Depot. This is the single most common reason a Toronto renovation cleanout stalls, and it is exactly the sort of load we handle in one visit instead. Verified against the City of Toronto Oversized & Metal Items page, July 2026.

What about carpet?

Carpet must be tied in rolls no heavier than 20 kilograms (44 pounds) and no longer than 1.2 metres (4 feet). If you're clearing a whole home's worth of broadloom, that is a lot of cutting and tying — we take it as-is. Verified against the City of Toronto Oversized & Metal Items page, July 2026.
