Our Protocol

The Keep & Cherish Protocol

Before anything is loaded, our crews stop and set aside what matters to the family.

  • Photographs, albums and letters
  • Legal paperwork — wills, deeds, policies, bank books
  • Jewellery, coins, cash and small valuables
  • Military medals, awards and heirlooms
  • Anything you have told us to watch for

Everything flagged goes into a secure box for the family to review. If you are not on site, we photograph it and contact you before we finish.

Donation-first & responsible disposal

Most families would rather see things rehomed than landfilled, so that is the default.

  • Usable furniture, housewares and clothing routed to local donation partners
  • Scrap metal to a recycler
  • Electronics to proper e-waste handling
  • Appliances with refrigerant sent for recovery
  • Only the genuine remainder to a licensed transfer station

Light interior demolition & fixture removal

Where the property needs more than clearing to be list-ready.

  • Carpet and underlay rip-out
  • Non-structural partition and shelving removal
  • Shed, deck and fence demolition
  • Appliance and fixture extraction
  • Debris removed in the same visit

Structural work, asbestos and anything requiring a permit are outside our scope — we will tell you plainly and point you to the right trade.

Broom-clean guarantee

We do not simply empty a property — we finish it.

  • Floors swept throughout
  • Surfaces wiped, cobwebs cleared
  • Garage and basement floors swept clean
  • Nothing left in any room, shed or yard
  • Ready for photography, showings or closing

Scheduling that respects the deadline

Estates run on closing dates and probate timelines.

For Executors & Families

Questions We're Asked Most

Can you work with an executor who lives out of town or out of province?

Yes — this is one of the most common situations we handle. You do not need to be present. We can assess the property from a video walkthrough, send you a written flat-rate quote by email, coordinate access with a realtor, lawyer or neighbour, and send you dated before-and-after photographs on completion. Many executors settle an entire property with us without ever being in the same province.

What happens if you find money, jewellery or important documents?

They are set aside for the family, every time. Our Keep & Cherish protocol means crews stop and flag anything that looks personal or valuable — cash, jewellery, bank books, deeds, wills, insurance papers, military medals, photographs — and set it aside in a secure box for you to review before anything leaves the property. Nothing of that nature is ever discarded or taken. If you are not on site, we photograph the items and contact you before we finish.

How quickly can you clear a three-floor home?

Most three-floor homes with a basement and garage are completed in two to three working days with a dedicated crew. Smaller properties are often a single day. Because we run high-capacity hydraulic dump trailers rather than pickup trucks, we move far more volume per trip, which is what keeps multi-day jobs from stretching into a week. We confirm the exact schedule in writing with your quote, and we work to closing dates.

Do we need to pack or sort anything before you arrive?

No. You can leave the property exactly as it is. If the family has already set aside items to keep, mark that room or tape off those items and we will not touch them. If nothing has been sorted, our crew does the sorting as part of the job — that is what the service is for. The one thing worth doing in advance, if you can, is telling us about anything specific you are hoping we find.

Is the property ready for listing photographs when you leave?

Yes. Every job finishes broom-clean: floors swept, surfaces wiped, cobwebs removed, nothing left behind in any room, garage, shed or basement. Realtors use us specifically so the property can be photographed or shown the next day. If you need a higher finish — degreased garage floors, deep-vacuumed basements — tell us at assessment and we will scope it in.

What happens to everything you remove?

Donation first. Usable furniture, housewares, clothing, books and small appliances are routed to local donation partners wherever they can be accepted, because most families would rather see those things rehomed than landfilled. Scrap metal goes to a recycler, electronics go to proper e-waste, and only the genuine remainder goes to a licensed transfer station. We can provide a summary of where things went if the estate requires it.
