The eastern edge, same standard

Pickering marks the eastern boundary of our service area, and it gets exactly what Toronto gets: the same crew, the same white-glove protocol, and the same price fixed on site before anything moves. From the established streets around Amberlea and Rougemount to the new builds rising in Seaton, the work varies but the standard doesn't.

Older homes, deeper storage

Pickering's established neighbourhoods were built with real basements and proper garages, and after twenty or thirty years those spaces hold a family's entire history. Clearing one is rarely a single-afternoon job for a homeowner with a hatchback — it's why the calls we get here tend to arrive after someone has already tried doing it themselves over three weekends.

New-build move-ins and renovation debris

Seaton and the newer pockets generate a different kind of work: packaging from a move, furniture that didn't survive the upgrade, and renovation debris from finishing a basement. We coordinate around contractors and leave the site broom-clean so the next trade isn't working around a pile.

Waterfront and lakeside properties

Homes along the Pickering waterfront often mean tighter access and longer carries. We plan the haul route before we start, protect the path, and carry further rather than risk a finish. It's slower and it's the right way to do it.

Durham timing is worth knowing

Durham's own large-item collection runs on a biweekly cycle with a two-item limit — workable for a single couch, not for a basement. The disposal questions below set out how the Region actually handles it, verified against Durham's own guidance.
