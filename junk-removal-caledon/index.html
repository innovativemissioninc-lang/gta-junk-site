Caledon is a different kind of cleanout

Most of the GTA is subdivisions and semis. Caledon is estate lots, hobby farms, century homes in Alton and Belfountain, and properties where the garage is a detached three-bay and there's a barn behind it. The volume on a single Caledon clearing routinely exceeds what an entire street in the city would produce, and the access is nothing like a driveway in Etobicoke.

That suits us. We plan the haul route before anything moves, bring the capacity to finish in one visit rather than shuttling loads, and protect gravel drives and finished floors alike on the way through.

Bolton, and why we're quicker here than you'd expect

Bolton is roughly twenty minutes from our base in northwest Etobicoke — genuinely closer than downtown Toronto. Same-day and next-day availability in Bolton is real rather than aspirational, which matters when a closing date or a tenant changeover is driving the timeline.

The older streets around Queen and King Street West bring the usual older-home challenges: narrow stairs to a finished basement, a detached garage with a single door, and thirty years of accumulation behind it. Newer builds south of the river tend to be volume rather than access problems. Both are one visit.

Rural properties, outbuildings and estate clearings

Barn clearings, workshop clear-outs, equipment sheds and long-held farm properties are some of the most satisfying work we do — and the most commonly mishandled. Scrap metal has value and should be separated, not landfilled. Usable tools and equipment deserve a second owner. Anything with fuel or chemicals in it needs a proper disposal channel rather than a truck bed. We sort for all three before a single load leaves.

Estate and downsizing work in Caledon

Caledon holds a lot of homes owned by the same family for decades, and clearing one is as much an emotional job as a physical one. Our crews sweep every room for photographs, documents and valuables before anything is loaded, and those go into a clean labelled bin for the family. Usable furniture is staged for Peel-area donation partners. The house is handed back showable for whatever comes next.

Caledon Disposal & Pricing Questions

Does Peel collect large appliances from the curb in Caledon?

No. Peel Region no longer collects large appliances from the curb anywhere in the Region, Caledon included. Fridges, stoves, washers and dryers must go to a Peel Community Recycling Centre — accepted free of charge — or be taken by your retailer on delivery of the replacement. Verified against Peel Region's Bulky Items page, July 2026.

What counts as a bulky item here?

Peel defines a bulky item as any household item larger than 1 metre (39 inches) in any one dimension or heavier than 20 kilograms (44 pounds), furniture included regardless of size. Acceptable bulky items are collected free from the curb on your regular garbage day if out by 7:00 a.m. Verified against Peel Region's Bulky Items page, July 2026.

Rural property — does that change anything?

Collection rules are Region-wide, but rural Caledon properties often mean long driveways, outbuildings and barn clearings that curbside collection was never designed for. That volume is exactly what a single-visit clearing handles. Drop-off fees apply for general bulky items at Peel CRCs. Verified against Peel Region's Bulky Items page, July 2026.
