Local Ranking & Growth Checklist

Everything that moves the needle on local visibility, in rough order of return per hour spent. Work top to bottom. Section 1 alone is worth more than sections 3 and 4 combined.

1. Google Business Profile — the single highest-leverage asset you own

In local junk removal, the map pack decides who gets called. Roughly three quarters of the clicks go to the top three. Everything below is free and most of it takes under an hour.

  • Primary category: set to Garbage Collection Service. This is the category that competes in the GTA map pack for junk and cleanout searches.
  • Secondary categories: add Waste Management Service, Demolition Contractor, Dumpster Rental Service (only once you actually offer it) and Debris Removal Service. Secondary categories widen the queries you surface for without diluting the primary.
  • Service area: list cities individually — Toronto, Etobicoke, Mississauga, Vaughan, Brampton, Markham, Richmond Hill, Ajax, Whitby, Oshawa, Newmarket, Milton. Do not set a 200 km radius; Google treats a huge radius as a weak relevance signal.
  • Services list: add every service as a separate entry with its own description and starting price — Estate Cleanout $2,000, Hoarding Cleanout $4,000, Pre-Listing Cleanout $1,500, Insurance & Restoration Debris $1,800, Commercial Cleanout $2,500, Garage Cleanout $2,000. Most competitors leave this blank.
  • Business description (750 chars): lead with "estate cleanouts" and "property cleanouts", name the GTA cities, mention the MECP registration. Do not keyword-stuff — write it for a person.
  • Photos — the lever almost nobody pulls: upload at least 5 new photos a week, forever. Profiles with regular photo uploads get materially more views. Geotag nothing; Google strips EXIF. Just upload consistently.
  • Photo types to shoot on every job: wide before, wide after, crew working, truck on site, a detail shot of the finished floor. That is five photos per job, which covers your whole week from one job.
  • Google Posts: publish one a week. Posts expire after seven days, so a stale profile is a visible signal. Rotate: a recent job, a service explainer, a seasonal prompt (spring cleanouts, pre-holiday clearing), a review screenshot.
  • Q&A: seed it yourself. Post 8–10 real questions from your own second Google account and answer them from the business account. "Do you do estate cleanouts?", "What does a hoarding cleanout cost?", "Do you take appliances?" This is explicitly allowed and almost no competitor does it.
  • Products: add each service tier as a Product with its price and a photo. Products show in the profile and reinforce the high-ticket positioning before anyone calls.
  • Attributes: tick everything that applies — "Online estimates", "Onsite services", "LGBTQ+ friendly", "Identifies as Black-owned" if you choose to use it (it surfaces a badge and drives real clicks).
  • Booking link: point it at /fast-quote/, not the homepage.
  • Messaging: turn it on only if you will answer inside an hour. Google penalises slow response, and an unanswered message is worse than no messaging.

2. Review velocity — the second-biggest ranking factor you control

Google weighs recency and rate, not just the total. Four reviews a month beats forty reviews from last year. The target is every completed job asked, same day.

  • Ask on site, at the handover. Walk the space with the client, get the "wow", then say: "If you are happy, the single most useful thing you could do for us is a Google review — I will text you the link right now while we are standing here." Ask-in-person converts several times better than a cold text.
  • Send the link within 10 minutes of leaving, while the cleared room is still the thing they are looking at. Same-day asks convert far better than next-day.
  • Your direct review link: https://g.page/r/CWUHyrYovKM_EAI/review — it opens the review box directly. Save it as a text replacement on your phone (iPhone: Settings → General → Keyboard → Text Replacement, shortcut "revlink").
  • One follow-up only, 48 hours later, and never a third. Two asks is persistence; three is harassment and gets you a one-star.
  • Reply to every review within 24 hours, positive or negative. Replies are indexed, so work the service and the city into the reply naturally — "glad the Etobicoke garage came back the way you wanted".
  • Never offer anything in exchange for a review. No discount, no draw, no gift card. It violates Google’s policy and in Canada the Competition Act amendments make incentivised reviews a real liability, not a theoretical one.
  • Ask the referrer too. An estate lawyer or agent who sent you the job can leave a review about working with you, and professional reviews carry disproportionate weight with other professionals reading the profile.
  • Target: 2–4 new reviews a month, every month. Consistency beats bursts — a sudden cluster after months of silence looks bought and can get filtered.

3. Directories and citations — do these in order

Citations matter less than they did for ranking, but they still matter for consistency, and several of these send real leads. Use the identical name, address format and phone everywhere — "GTA Junk and Property Cleanout Inc." every single time.

  • HomeStars — the one that actually sends GTA leads in this trade. Claim the profile, complete it fully, and push your first 5 reviews here as well as Google. Paid tiers exist; do not pay until the free profile has reviews on it.
  • Bing Places — free, takes 15 minutes, and imports directly from your Google profile. Bing feeds Microsoft Copilot and Duck Duck Go local results.
  • Apple Business Connect — free. Feeds Apple Maps and Siri. Almost no junk removal company in the GTA has claimed theirs.
  • Yelp Canada — claim it, complete it, then largely ignore it. Do not solicit Yelp reviews; their filter aggressively suppresses solicited ones and it can hurt you.
  • Better Business Bureau — accreditation is paid and optional, but the free listing is worth claiming for the name consistency alone.
  • Yellow Pages Canada (yellowpages.ca) — free basic listing. Still ranks for "junk removal [city]" long-tail terms.
  • 411.ca, Cylex Canada, N49, Canpages, Opendi, Brownbook, Hotfrog — free aggregator citations. Do them in one sitting with a spreadsheet of your NAP details open. Two hours, done once.
  • Nextdoor — claim the business page for Etobicoke/Rexdale. Neighbourhood recommendations convert extremely well for cleanout work and cost nothing.
  • Houzz — worth it only if you start posting the garage transformation photos. The audience is renovation-adjacent and higher-ticket.
  • Trustindex — not a directory; it is a widget that pulls your Google reviews onto the website. Install the free tier once you have 5+ reviews and embed it on /reviews/ and the estate page. Social proof on the page you send ad traffic to is worth more than another citation.
  • Ontario Chamber / Etobicoke BIA — a local chamber membership buys you a high-authority local backlink and introductions to property managers. Check the cost against one estate job.
  • Consistency check: once a quarter, search your phone number in quotes on Google and fix any listing showing an old address, the old email, or a different business name.

4. Backlinks and local authority — slow, but it is the moat

Citations get you listed. Links get you ranked. Five good local links beat five hundred directory entries.

  • Charity partners: ask the GTA charities you donate to for a "donation partners" mention with a link. Furniture Bank, Habitat ReStore, Diabetes Canada. This is the most natural link in your business and it is free.
  • Estate law firms and brokerages you work with: ask for a "trusted trades" or "resources" listing on their site once you have completed a job for them. Lead with the referral fee conversation, not the link.
  • Local news and community: an Etobicoke community paper will cover a genuine local story — free cleanouts for a family in hardship, a large donation run. Do the thing first, then tell them about it.
  • Supplier and association pages: any transfer station, recycler or equipment supplier with a partner list.
  • Guest content: write the "how to clear a parent’s estate in Ontario" piece for a real estate brokerage’s blog. You already have the expertise written up on /estate-cleanouts/.

7. The weekly rhythm

If you only do one thing, do this. Thirty minutes a week, every week, beats a ten-hour burst twice a year.

  • After every job: 5 photos uploaded to Google Business Profile, review link texted within 10 minutes.
  • Monday, 15 minutes: one Google Post published. Reply to any new reviews.
  • Friday, 15 minutes: check GA4 for which pages are converting, check Search Console for new queries you are surfacing for.
  • First of the month: one new directory or citation claimed from section 3. One partner contacted from section 4.
  • Quarterly: NAP consistency check. Review the starting prices against what jobs are actually costing you to deliver.
