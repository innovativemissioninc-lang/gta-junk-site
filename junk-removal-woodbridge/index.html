The closest market we serve, and one of the busiest

Woodbridge sits about four kilometres north of our base in northwest Etobicoke — closer to us than most of Toronto is. That proximity is not a marketing line; it's the difference between "we'll fit you in Thursday" and "we can be there this afternoon."

Big houses, bigger garages

Woodbridge builds generously. Triple garages, finished basements, and the kind of storage that quietly absorbs two decades of accumulation before anyone notices the cars have been outside since the kids were small. Vellore and Sonoma Heights in particular are full of newer homes where the garage became storage during the move and never recovered.

These are our favourite jobs because the transformation is dramatic and the finish shows. A Woodbridge garage taken to bare, degreased concrete photographs beautifully — which matters more than you'd think when the house eventually goes to market.

Older Woodbridge, along Islington and Market Lane

The established streets bring different problems: narrower drives, detached garages with a single door, mature trees making access tight, and basements reached by stairs that were never designed for a three-seat sofa. We plan the haul route before anything moves and carry further rather than risk a wall.

Downsizing and estate work

Woodbridge holds a lot of homes owned by the same family for thirty or forty years, often with multiple generations under one roof. Clearing one is emotional work as much as physical. Our crews check every drawer for photographs and documents before a box goes in the truck, and those go to the family in a clean labelled bin — never into the load.

Woodbridge Disposal & Pricing Questions

Where do bulky items and appliances go in York Region?

York Region runs Community Environmental Centres that take bulky items and large appliances — the Vaughan CEC at 130 McCleary Court is the closest to Woodbridge. Disposal fees apply on items dropped as garbage. Verified against York Region's Waste Depots pages, July 2026.

What if it's still in good condition?

York Region encourages donating usable items to charity rather than disposing of them, which matches how we work — anything serviceable is staged for local donation partners before disposal is even considered. Verified against York Region's waste guidance, July 2026.
