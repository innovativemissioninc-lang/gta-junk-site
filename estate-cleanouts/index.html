Clearing a parent’s house is not a junk removal job

An estate cleanout is forty years of a life, and somewhere in it are the things the family actually wants — the photographs, the marriage certificate, the ring in the sock drawer, the bank statements the lawyer needs. A general junk crew is paid to move volume. They will not slow down for a shoebox of negatives, and once a truck is tipped at a transfer station there is no recovering anything.

We work the other way around. Every estate job is sorted before it is loaded: documents, photographs, jewellery and anything that looks like it mattered go into a labelled bin for the family to review. Only then does the rest move. That is slower, it costs more, and it is the entire reason executors hire us instead of the cheapest quote.

We also understand the position an executor is in. You have a legal duty to the beneficiaries, you often live somewhere else, and you need the property emptied and showable before a listing date you did not choose. We work to that date, send photographs at each stage, and give you a written fixed price you can put in front of the other beneficiaries without having to defend it.

Good Questions

Estate Cleanout — Questions We Get Asked

How much does an estate cleanout cost in Toronto?

Estate cleanouts start at $1,200 for a standard clear and $2,500 for our Signature finish, which includes deep cleaning, Keep & Cherish item delivery and a photographed handover. The exact number depends on the size of the property, how full it is, access, and how far you want the finish taken. It is fixed in writing at a free on-site valuation before a single item moves, and jobs over $2,000 take a 30% deposit that comes off the final invoice. We do not quote estates blind over the phone — we would have to pad the number, and you would be paying for our guesswork.

Can you do the cleanout if I live outside Ontario?

Yes, and it is common. We arrange access through a realtor, lawyer or lockbox, send you a photographed walkthrough before anything moves, confirm by text what is being set aside, and send a second photographed set at completion. Several of our estate clients have never been in the property. Invoicing and payment are handled remotely.

What happens to photographs, documents and jewellery?

They are recovered and set aside, never loaded. Every room is sorted before it is cleared, and anything that reads as personal — papers, photo albums, negatives, jewellery boxes, cash, medals, documents — goes into labelled bins for the family. On a Signature cleanout you also get a written list of anything that appeared to have value. If the family wants those items delivered rather than left on site, we deliver them anywhere in the GTA.

How quickly can you clear an estate property before a listing date?

Most GTA estate properties are cleared in one to three working days once the job is booked, and estate work gets our priority scheduling. If you have a firm listing date, tell us at the valuation and we will book backwards from it so the agent can photograph the house the morning after we finish.

Do you provide documentation for the estate accounting?

Yes. You get dated before-and-after photographs, scale tickets for anything priced by weight, donation receipts where the receiving charity issues them, and a single itemised invoice. Executors routinely file this with the estate accounts, and estate lawyers who refer us ask for it by default.

Are you licensed and insured to do this work?

Yes. We are registered with Ontario’s Ministry of the Environment, Conservation and Parks as a waste transportation system — registration number R-004-5477911257 — and we carry $2 million in commercial general liability insurance. Certificates of insurance are available on request, which estate lawyers and property managers often need for their file.

What will you not take?

We are registered for non-hazardous waste, so we cannot transport asbestos-containing material, biomedical or biohazardous waste, PCBs, radioactive material, fuels, propane cylinders or unidentified chemical containers. If something like that turns up in an estate — and in older homes it sometimes does — we stop, tell you, and point you to the correctly licensed contractor rather than quietly putting it in the truck.

Do you buy the contents or take them in trade?

No, and we would be cautious of anyone who offers to. We are paid to clear the property, which means we have no interest in what anything is worth. If we find something that looks genuinely valuable we flag it to you and recommend an appraiser. An operator who both values the contents and keeps them has an obvious conflict with the estate.
