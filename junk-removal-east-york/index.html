Small houses, deep storage

East York is compact by design — wartime bungalows and post-war semis on tidy lots. What the frontage doesn't tell you is that most have full basements, and after fifty years those basements are the densest storage in the city. The volume that comes out of an East York bungalow routinely surprises the owner.

Leaside is a different job again

Leaside's larger family homes, finished basements and detached garages mean a whole-property clearing rather than a single room, often ahead of a sale in a market where presentation genuinely moves the price. Those get our most thorough finish — degreased garage floor, deep-vacuumed corners, ready for a photographer.

Downsizing, and a lot of it

East York has one of the higher concentrations of long-term owners now moving to condos or closer to family. Those clearings need patience: keep, family, donate, go — decided room by room, at the owner's pace, with nothing personal going near the truck until they've seen it.

Ravine lots and awkward access

Homes backing onto the Don Valley bring long stair runs, steep side yards and no vehicle access to the rear. We plan for it. It costs us time and it costs you nothing extra, because the price is fixed before we start.

Good Questions

East York Disposal & Pricing Questions

Will the City take my old couch and fridge?

Yes. Toronto collects oversized items — appliances, furniture, carpets — on your regular garbage day, set 0.5 metres from your bins and out by 7:00 a.m. Furniture doesn't need dismantling, but doors must come off fridges and ovens. Verified against the City of Toronto Oversized & Metal Items page, July 2026.

What won't they take?

Construction and renovation waste — drywall, wood, and similar — isn't collected at the curb, and neither is hazardous waste like paint or fluorescent tubes. Those need a City Drop-Off Depot. That's the most common reason a renovation clearing stalls here. Verified against the City of Toronto Oversized & Metal Items page, July 2026.
