Most demolition quotes leave out the part that costs money

Taking a shed or a garage down is the easy half. The expensive half is what is standing in the driveway afterwards: a twenty-cubic-yard pile of framing, shingles, siding and concrete that a demolition crew with no hauling capacity will quote you separately for, or simply leave.

We run a heavy-duty commercial hydraulic dump trailer, which means the teardown and the haul are one mobilisation and one price. The structure comes down, the material is separated as it is loaded — metal to a recycler, clean wood and shingles routed correctly — and the pad is swept. One crew, one day on most jobs, one invoice.

We also handle the permit. Most GTA municipalities require a demolition permit for a detached structure above a certain footprint, and an addition or anything attached to the dwelling effectively always needs one. We confirm what your municipality requires and obtain it before work begins. A contractor who offers to skip that is handing you the liability, not saving you money.

Good Questions

Light Structural Demolition — Questions We Get Asked

How much does it cost to demolish a shed or garage in Toronto?

Light structural demolition runs $1,800 to $4,500. A straightforward backyard shed sits at the bottom of that range; a double garage with a concrete slab, poor access and a tight property line sits at the top. The variables are size, construction, how much of it is concrete, how far the material has to be carried to the trailer, and whether the slab and footings come out. The permit fee is passed through at cost. Price is fixed in writing at a free on-site assessment, and a 30% deposit confirms the booking.

Do I need a demolition permit, and will you get it?

In most GTA municipalities, yes for anything attached to the house and yes for detached structures above the local footprint threshold — the threshold itself varies, which is why we check rather than guess. We confirm the requirement with your municipality and obtain the permit before work begins. We will not start without one; an unpermitted demolition is a problem that surfaces at closing when the buyer’s lawyer asks for it.

What about asbestos in an older garage?

It comes up regularly in structures built before 1990 — cement-board siding, roofing felt, panel board and old floor tile are the usual places. Where the Occupational Health and Safety Act requires a designated substance survey, we tell you before quoting and the abatement is done by a licensed abatement contractor, not by us. Our MECP registration covers non-hazardous waste only, so we physically cannot haul it. We would rather lose the job than load it.

Do you remove the concrete slab and footings?

Yes, priced as a separate line item so you can decide. Concrete is heavy and goes by the tonne with scale tickets provided, which is why we never bury it inside a lump-sum number. Some clients leave the slab for a shed rebuild or a parking pad; others want the pad gone and the ground re-graded.

How long does a demolition take?

Most sheds are a single day. A single-car garage is typically one to two days, a double garage or a small addition two to three, and longer if the slab is coming out or access is poor. We give you the schedule at the assessment and we book around your closing or listing date if you have one.

Do you disconnect the hydro and gas?

We confirm disconnection before any structure comes down, and where a licensed trade or the utility has to do it, we coordinate that and will not proceed until it is verified. A detached garage with a live sub-panel is one of the more common things we find that the homeowner had forgotten about.
