The sand is the job, not the pool

Water is drained to what the City of Toronto permits at a rate the ground can absorb — a 24-foot pool holds roughly 50,000 litres.

People underestimate these consistently, and so do a lot of contractors. A standard 24-foot above-ground pool sits on three to six tonnes of sand. Add the steel wall, liner, frame, surround deck, pump housing and paving stones and you are well past what a pickup and a weekend will handle. We run a commercial hydraulic dump trailer, so the base comes out in bulk rather than in bags — which is the single biggest reason this costs less with us than with an outfit running a half-tonne.

Draining in Toronto has rules

A 24-foot pool holds roughly 50,000 litres. Releasing that onto a lawn in an afternoon is how you put water into your own basement or your neighbour’s. We drain in a controlled way to what the City of Toronto permits, dechlorinated where required, at a rate the ground can actually absorb. We check the local requirement before we open a valve.

Side-yard access on a Toronto lot

Most Toronto backyards are reached by a side yard a metre wide, or through the house, or not at all without lifting a fence panel. We plywood-track over soft ground, lift and replace fence panels where that is the only route, and protect the lawn on the way in and out. Tight access is the normal case here, not an exception.

Worth doing before you list

Agents ask us for this constantly. An unused above-ground pool reads to a buyer as a maintenance cost and a safety question, and it hides the usable yard in listing photographs. Clearing it and levelling the ground reliably shows better than leaving it. Tell us your MLS date and we will book backwards from it.
